Glossary of Terms

Demystifying bookkeeping jargon so you can understand your business health.

Accounts Payable (AP)
Money that a business owes to its suppliers or vendors for goods or services received on credit. It is recorded as a liability on the balance sheet.
Accounts Receivable (AR)
Money owed to a business by its customers for goods or services delivered but not yet paid for. It is recorded as an asset.
Accrual Basis Accounting
An accounting method where revenue and expenses are recorded when they are incurred, regardless of when cash is officially exchanged.
Balance Sheet
A core financial statement that summarizes a company's assets, liabilities, and shareholders' equity at a specific point in time.
Cash Basis Accounting
An accounting method where revenues and expenses are recorded only when cash actually enters or leaves the bank account.
Chart of Accounts (COA)
An organized index of all the financial accounts in the general ledger of a company. It provides a map to how transactions are categorized.
Depreciation
The process of allocating the cost of a physical asset over its useful life. It represents how much an asset's value has been utilized.
General Ledger (GL)
The master set of accounts that summarizes all transactions occurring within an entity. It serves as the primary source for financial statements.
Net Income
Also known as the "bottom line," this is a company's total revenue minus all of its expenses, taxes, and costs during a specific period.
Reconciliation
The process of comparing internal financial records (like your ledger) against external records (like a bank statement) to ensure they match perfectly.
Profit and Loss Statement (P&L)
A financial statement that summarizes the revenues, costs, and expenses incurred during a specific period, usually a fiscal quarter or year.
Trial Balance
A bookkeeping worksheet in which the balances of all ledgers are compiled into equal debit and credit account column totals.
